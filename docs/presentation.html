Project concept

The generated
Backrooms

Empty yellow rooms share one worn wallpaper style. Seeded layouts vary across 6, 12 and 18 m footprints under a 3.2 m ceiling.

Explore alone with recorded footsteps and a blurred camera view. Optional flashlight clues lead toward an exit.

Version 0.8: solitary exploration at a slower pace

Procedural generation

Endless exploration,
bounded active geometry

25

playable chunks at radius 2

Each chunk contains 6 × 6 cells.

Prepare one mesh per frame in an outer ring of up to 24 chunks. Crossing a boundary promotes cached geometry.

Seed + coordinates reproduce the base layout. Numeric ranges remain finite.

Implementation: world.cpp

Generation rule

A connected route
through every cell

Every non-origin cell reserves an opening toward a neighbour closer to the origin.

distance = abs(x) + abs(z)

Each parent step decreases this distance. Optional openings add variation without removing reserved routes.

A shared edge uses the same decision from either adjacent cell

Viewing pipeline

Local geometry
becomes a camera view

clip = projection × view × model × position

Yaw and pitch define orientation. Vertical FOV eases from 78° to 82° during Shift movement. Crouching lowers the eye height.

A nearby rendering origin keeps GPU coordinates small. Acceleration and braking give the slower movement a sense of weight.

Course references: L-4-2 Models, pp. 4–20; L-5-1 Viewing, pp. 3–22 and 36–47

Fragment shading

Lighting establishes
the atmosphere

diffuse = max(dot(N, L), 0)
specular = pow(max(dot(N, H), 0), shininess)

Nearby fluorescent fixtures and a flashlight illuminate the yellow rooms. Attenuation limits their range, while fog softens distant geometry.

Course references: L-5-2 Shading, pp. 14–32; L-7 Buffers, pp. 47–48

Materials

Worn wallpaper
and camera blur

Generated wallpaper contains scars, scuffs and peeling patches. Wall coordinates fix the marks in place. Mipmaps soften distant detail.

The offscreen scene receives stronger blur, grain and vignette.

The interface draws afterward, so text stays sharp. V shows a minimal HUD.

The camera effect changes pixels. It does not record video.

Course reference: L-8 Texture Mapping, pp. 25–47

Distinctive interaction

Movement and
interaction

Walking reaches 2.5 m/s, Shift movement 4.0 m/s and crouching 1.1 m/s. Velocity builds gradually and settles when input stops.

Steps follow actual travel. The optional exit checks both distance and a clear path. Enter resumes exploration after escape.

Six recorded footfall variants replace the synthetic steps. Crouching stays quieter. Source: GboxMikeFozzy, OpenGameArt, CC0. Full credit: THIRD_PARTY.md.

Collision prevents crossing solid walls and activating the exit through them
