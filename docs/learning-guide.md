# Version 0.12 code walkthrough and defence preparation

Read the README, then find each concept in the source. Change a small parameter, predict its effect, rebuild and compare the result with your prediction.

1. **Geometry.** Trace a wall from cell coordinates to triangles, a vertex buffer and a draw call. Explain positions, normals, UVs and winding order.
2. **Connectivity.** Find the reserved parent edge in `world.cpp`. Explain why decreasing `abs(x) + abs(z)` reaches the origin, why optional openings preserve that path, and how adjacent cells agree on a shared edge.
3. **Room categories.** Follow the rules for classic rooms, corridors, open halls, pillar halls, dead ends and odd rooms. Identify the 7.5-metre cells, 7.5/15/22.5-metre room footprints, 45-metre chunks and uniform 3.2-metre ceiling. Explain how pillar placement and optional doors preserve reserved connecting routes, and why the single creature must query the same collision geometry as the player.
4. **Streaming.** Explain the 25 playable chunks and outer ring of at most 24 prepared neighbours. Follow `prepareAhead`, cache promotion and eviction. Explain floor division for negative coordinates and why a teleport can require synchronous generation. Find the GPU fences and distinguish limiting queued work from raising average FPS.
5. **Camera.** Trace `SmoothLook` in `common/player_state.h`, yaw/pitch, delta time, view matrix and perspective projection. Explain time-based exponential smoothing, matrix order and camera-relative rendering coordinates. Follow the default 78° to 82° FOV transition, travel-based head bob and crouching from 1.65 to 1.08 metres.
6. **Heavy movement and stamina.** In `common/gameplay.h`, `common/movement.h` and `common/player_state.h`, find movement targets, acceleration/braking and stamina. Explain normalized diagonals, actual-travel sprint drain, recovery delay, exhaustion threshold and pause/reset behavior. Explain why holding Shift against a wall should not drain stamina.
7. **Collision.** Follow movement into wall tests. Explain the player radius, small collision-checked steps and sliding. Why should pushing into a wall produce neither continued travel nor repeated footstep events?
8. **Lighting.** Identify `max(dot(N,L),0)`, the halfway vector, specular exponent, attenuation and flashlight cone. Trace seeded working/dying/off states and the shared `lampEnvelope` calculation. Explain how each fixture's packed material phase keeps its glow synchronized with point-light power. Confirm the light capacity and common coordinate space, and explain the lack of wall-occlusion tests.
9. **Textures and normals.** Follow generated colour pixels and physical-height fields through OpenGL upload and mipmaps. Trace finite differences into tangent-space normal maps and the derivative-based tangent basis in the fragment shader. Explain why floor/ceiling orientation matters, why normal textures use linear RGB, and how damp carpet changes appearance. For the wallpaper scars, explain why the origin phase uses 180 metres: it is a common multiple of the 12-metre sheet and 45-metre chunk. Distinguish shading relief from additional geometry or damage physics.
10. **Postprocessing.** Trace the offscreen framebuffer through `fshader_lens.glsl`. Explain the lightly softened 13-sample image, reduced default radius of `0.8 + 0.3 * edge` reference pixels, adjustable softness and sharp later UI pass. Distinguish pixel processing from moving geometry and from video recording.
11. **Spatial sound.** Follow bundled footstep WAV files through loading, surface filtering and the cached stereo PCM mixer. Trace travelled distance into steps, and world-space source/listener positions into panning and 3D distance attenuation. Explain seeded distant ambience, the environmental tension drone/stinger, the voice limit and the absence of HRTF/wall occlusion. Explain why pausing, wall pushing and resetting should not produce bursts of steps. Use the audio preview and locate the recordings' credit in `THIRD_PARTY.md`.
12. **Door animation and interaction.** Trace `doorPose`, `advanceDoors`, `doorVertices` and `exitCrossed` in `world.cpp`. Explain the hinge position, 90-degree rotation, easing over 0.8 seconds, shared visual/collision pose, blocked closing reversal and animation pause. Locate the farther wall aperture at `(6.225, -267)` and its approach `(6.225, -265.8)`; explain why E only opens its leaf and the player must walk through it to escape. Follow bounded opened-door memory and continuing after the ending.
13. **Menus and soundtrack.** In `common/settings.h`, follow parsing, bounds validation and saving; compare window mouse coordinates with framebuffer-space menu rectangles. Explain which state persists and which resets. In `common/music.h` and `.cpp`, trace the user-supplied Temporex track through Windows MCI, separate music/master volume, looping and pause/resume without rewinding. Distinguish this background track from spatial sounds and from the generated audio-preview WAV. Explain why local asset conversion is not a runtime dependency, and identify the supplied track's attribution in `THIRD_PARTY.md`.

14. **Imported creature and animation.** Follow the supplied `source/Zombie Scream.fbx` through its offline conversion into `assets/models/smiler/smiler.brm`. Identify the original 4,466-vertex, 8,728-triangle mesh, 65-bone skeleton and scream clip, then distinguish the added walk/run cycles and integration code. In `common/threat_model.cpp`, trace cached clip sampling/interpolation into vertex positions, normals and body/eye/tooth materials. Distinguish the offline skeletal deformation from runtime vertex-frame interpolation; this implementation does not evaluate a GPU bone hierarchy. Explain how movement speed selects walking versus running, why pose phase should remain continuous, and how paused simulation freezes it. Follow camera-relative model transforms, the capture eye anchor and near-camera placement. Compare standing and low-clearance poses through `World::headClearance`. Explain why importing a mesh is different from authoring its geometry, and why Blender is an offline conversion tool rather than a runtime dependency.
